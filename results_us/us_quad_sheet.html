Inflation: what is behind the numbers

  • Next print, Sep 2026: +0.39% on the month, 3.45% year over year (up from 3.35%). Gasoline adds +0.20pp (pump prices are +4.4% this month so far), rents +0.08pp, everything else +0.10pp.
  • Then a peak, then a big fall. Inflation tops out at 3.58% in Nov 2026 with oil high, and falls to 1.92% by May 2027. The fall is arithmetic more than forecast: the big monthly price rises of spring 2026 drop out of the 12-month comparison one by one. Those months carry the sheet's strongest calls.
  • Oil. The rule of thumb is $1 on a barrel of oil ≈ 0.03 percentage points on inflation (Hedgeye's number; the data since 2000 say 0.031). Oil is $97 now against $84 in Aug 2026, and the pump price $4.24 a gallon. That is what makes Sep 2026 hot.
  • Rents follow new-lease rents (Zillow) with about a year's lag; those are up 2.3%, so rents add a steady 0.08pp a month. Wages are up 3.1%, which feeds services prices slowly.

Growth: what is behind the numbers

  • This quarter (Q3 2026): about +2.4% annualized, estimated from jobs, industrial production, retail sales and jobless claims with 1 of 3 months in. (Annualized = the pace over a full year if the quarter repeated.)
  • After this quarter we do not forecast GDP. Nobody can, two to four quarters out; every method tried did worse than a constant. So the sheet assumes the economy's normal pace, 2.0% a year, from the quarter after next, adjusted for interest rates as below. (The old setting, the last six years' typical pace of 3.1%, is backward looking: it assumes the post-2020 boom continues.)
  • Up or down is decided by the bar to beat. Year-over-year growth rises in a quarter only if that quarter grows faster than the same quarter a year earlier. Those bars are already published, so each quarter's call is "does our assumed pace clear a known bar". The table shows the bar, our path, and what a slower economy would do to the quad.

Interest rates

  • Where rates are. The Fed funds rate was 3.64% through mid-September; the September hike takes it to about 3.88%, and the market expects 4.63% by July 2027, roughly three more hikes.
  • How the sheet uses that. Rate hikes slow growth with a lag: in the data since 1960, growth is lower for the four quarters that start two quarters after the hikes, by about 0.4pp of annual growth per 1pp of hikes. So each quarter's assumed pace is the trend plus the effect of rate changes over the year ending two quarters earlier.
  • What that means now. The Fed cut 1.6pp over the last two years, and under that lag the cuts still help growth through Q1 2027. The new hikes start to bite in Q3 2027, at −0.3 to −0.4pp of annualized growth, and first change a quad in Q1 2027.

Your view: the Fed keeps hiking, and growth slows in Q1 and Q2 2027

If that is right, both quarters are Quad 4, because inflation is falling hard in both anyway (−0.33pp and −0.95pp). What growth has to do: come in under +2.1% annualized in Q1 2027 and under +1.5% in Q2 2027. The bars are low because early 2026 was weak, so it does not take a recession, just a real slowdown.

What the sheet says. Its own path is +2.2% in Q1 2027 and +1.9% in Q2 2027, against bars of +2.1% and +1.5%: Quad 1 (too close to call) and Quad 1. Q1 2027 is a margin of +0.2pp, so it is a coin flip whichever way the data lean. Q2 2027 is a margin of +0.5pp; a hike at every meeting with the fastest response on record takes about 0.5pp off it, which is enough to tip it to Quad 4 on its own. Hikes take at least two quarters to bite, and the 2024–26 cuts are still working in the other direction; so the timing, not the size, is what decides it.

What would confirm your view early: jobless claims rising through the autumn, retail sales going flat, the Q4 2026 GDP print (28 January 2027) coming in under +0.5% annualized, and the Q1 2027 print (late April) under +2.1%. The consumer lines above are where it would show first: real income is already weak and the saving rate is already very low.

Honesty note. In the 2017–2026 backtest the rate effect made the growth calls slightly worse, not better: it reversed 42 of 540 calls and was right on 24% of those against 76% without it. Both times it mattered (2018, 2023) the Fed hiked into an economy that kept growing. It is on because you asked for it; the cards above say where it changes a quad.

How to read the growth levels. After this quarter they are an assumption (the economy's normal pace, 2.0% a year, from the quarter after next, plus the rate effect), not a forecast. What the sheet stands behind is the direction, up or down against the bar, and the inflation path.

Caveats. The backtest uses simulated first releases of GDP (the real-time archives are unreachable from where this is built), which costs it 5–8 points of growth accuracy.
